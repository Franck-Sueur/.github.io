Research interests

My research concerns the analysis and controllability of partial differential equations. A large part of my work is motivated by mathematical fluid mechanics, in particular the Euler and Navier–Stokes equations, fluid-structure interactions, multiscale phenomena, asymptotic analysis, and the control of flows by suitable external actions.

I am also interested in PDEs arising from physics, including magnetohydrodynamics and micromagnetism, and in methods related to Runge-type approximation, unique continuation, and fractional elliptic problems.

Research themes

Analysis of PDEs from fluid mechanics

This direction concerns incompressible and viscous fluid models, especially the Euler and Navier–Stokes equations. The main questions involve well-posedness, qualitative properties of solutions, asymptotic regimes, singular limits, and multiscale phenomena.

Fluid-structure interaction

This theme studies the motion of rigid bodies, particles, filaments, and loops immersed in incompressible fluids. A recurring problem is to derive effective dynamics when some geometric parameter tends to zero, for instance in zero-radius or slender-body limits.

Controllability of PDEs and fluid flows

This direction investigates how a PDE system can be driven toward a desired configuration by means of boundary controls, distributed controls, or remote actions. It includes small-time controllability problems and Lagrangian controllability questions for fluid flows.

Approximation and fractional problems

Recent directions include Runge-type approximation for fluid equations and representation or variational formulas related to fractional elliptic equations.
