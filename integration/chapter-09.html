Chapter 9
Integrals with parameters

This chapter studies parameter-dependent integrals. The first result gives a criterion for the continuity of

t↦∫ Ef(x,t)dμ(x),

and the second gives a criterion for differentiating such a map under the integral sign. In both cases the main tool is dominated convergence, used through sequential characterizations of continuity and differentiability.

We consider a measurable set E in a measure space (X,A,μ), an interval I ⊂ ℝ, and a function

f : E × I → ℝ.

We are interested in the regularity, with respect to the parameter t, of the map

F : I → ℝ,t↦∫ Ef(x,t)dμ(x). (9.1)

Whenever we need to regard a function defined on E as a function on the whole space X, we extend it by 0 outside E without changing the notation. In particular, saying that a function is integrable over E means that this zero extension belongs to L1(X,A,μ).

9.1 Continuity under the integral sign

Theorem 9.1 (Continuity under the integral sign). Let t0 in I. We assume that

1.
there exists η > 0 such that, for every t in I satisfying |t − t0| < η, the function
x↦f(x,t)

is measurable on E and integrable over E,

2.
for μ-almost all x in E,
f(x,t) → f(x,t0)as t → t0,
3.
there exists a function g: E → [0,∞), integrable over E, such that for every t in I satisfying |t − t0| < η,
|f(x,t)|≤ g(x)

for μ-almost all x in E.

Then the function x↦f(x,t0) is integrable over E and

lim ⁡ t→t0 ∫ Ef(x,t)dμ(x) = ∫ Ef(x,t0)dμ(x).

Proof. Let (tn)n≥1 be a sequence in I converging to t0. By discarding finitely many terms, we may assume that

|tn − t0| < η

for all n ≥ 1.

For every n ≥ 1, hypothesis 3 provides a negligible set Nn ⊂ E such that

|f(x,tn)|≤ g(x)

for all x in E ∖ Nn. Hypothesis 2 provides a negligible set N∞ ⊂ E such that

f(x,tn) → f(x,t0)as n → +∞

for all x in E ∖ N∞. By countable stability of negligible sets, there exists M in A such that

N∞∪⋃ n≥1Nn ⊂ M,μ(M) = 0.

We define functions on X by

un(x) := f(x,tn)1E(x),u(x) := f(x,t0)1E(x),v(x) := g(x)1E(x).

The function v is in L1(μ) and is nonnegative. Moreover, for every x in X ∖ M, convergence and domination hold; outside E all zero extensions vanish. Thus, for every x in X ∖ M, we have

un(x) → u(x)as n → +∞,and|un(x)|≤ v(x)

for all n ≥ 1. Theorem 8.12 therefore yields

∫ Xundμ →∫ Xudμas n → +∞.

By the indicator formulation of the integral, this is exactly

∫ Ef(x,tn)dμ(x) →∫ Ef(x,t0)dμ(x)as n → +∞.

Since this is true for every sequence (tn) converging to t0, the sequential criterion for continuity proves the result. □

Corollary 9.2 (Continuity of derivatives under the integral sign). Let I ⊂ ℝ be an interval. We assume that

1.
for every t in I, the function x↦f(x,t) is measurable on E and integrable over E,
2.
for μ-almost all x in E, the map
t↦f(x,t)

is continuous on I,

3.
there exists a measurable set Z ⊂ E with μ(E ∖ Z) = 0 and there exists a function g: E → [0,∞), integrable over E, such that
|f(x,t)|≤ g(x)

for every x in Z and every t in I.

Then the map

t↦∫ Ef(x,t)dμ(x)

is continuous on I.

Proof. We apply Theorem 9.1 at each point t0 in I. □

9.2 Differentiation under the integral sign

Theorem 9.3 (Differentiation under the integral sign). Let I ⊂ ℝ be an open interval and let t0 in I. We assume that

1.
there exists η > 0 such that, with
J :=]t0 − η,t0 + η[,

one has J ⊂ I, and for every t in J the function

x↦f(x,t)

is measurable on E and integrable over E,

2.
for every t in J, the function
x↦∂f ∂t (x,t)

is measurable on E, and there exists a measurable set Z ⊂ E with μ(E ∖ Z) = 0 such that, for every x in Z, the map

t↦f(x,t)

is differentiable on J,

3.
there exists a function g: E → [0,∞), integrable over E, such that
|∂f ∂t (x,t) | ≤ g(x)

for every x in Z and every t in J.

Then the function

x↦∂f ∂t (x,t0)

is integrable over E, the map F defined in (9.1) is differentiable at t0, and

F′(t 0) = ∫ E∂f ∂t (x,t0)dμ(x).

Proof. Step 1. Sequential reduction. Let (tn)n≥1 be a sequence in I ∖{t0} converging to t0. By discarding finitely many terms, we may assume that

|tn − t0| < η

for every n ≥ 1.

Let Z be the full-measure subset of E given by hypotheses 2 and 3, and choose M in A such that

E ∖ Z ⊂ M,μ(M) = 0.

Step 2. Difference quotients and domination. For x in X, we define

un(x) := f(x,tn) − f(x,t0) tn − t0 1E(x),u(x) := ∂f ∂t (x,t0)1E(x),v(x) := g(x)1E(x).

For every x in Z, differentiability at t0 gives

un(x) → u(x)as n → +∞.

Moreover, for every x in Z and every n ≥ 1, the mean value theorem provides a point

sn(x) ∈] min ⁡(tn,t0), max ⁡(tn,t0)[⊂ J

such that

f(x,tn) − f(x,t0) tn − t0 = ∂f ∂t (x,sn(x)).

Therefore

|un(x)|≤ v(x)

for every x in Z and every n ≥ 1.

Step 3. Application of dominated convergence. On X ∖ M, convergence and domination hold, and outside E all zero extensions vanish. Since v is in L1(μ) and is nonnegative, Theorem 8.12 applied to the sequence (un)n≥1 yields

∫ Xundμ →∫ Xudμas n → +∞.

Step 4. Identification of the derivative. By the indicator formulation,

∫ Xundμ = 1 tn − t0 (∫ Ef(x,tn)dμ(x) −∫ Ef(x,t0)dμ(x)) = F(tn) − F(t0) tn − t0 ,

and

∫ Xudμ = ∫ E∂f ∂t (x,t0)dμ(x).

Therefore

F(tn) − F(t0) tn − t0 →∫ E∂f ∂t (x,t0)dμ(x)as n → +∞.

Since this is true for every sequence (tn) in I ∖{t0} converging to t0, the sequential characterization of the derivative proves the result. □

Corollary 9.4 (Smoothness under the integral sign). Let I ⊂ ℝ be an open interval. We assume that

1.
for every t in I, the function x↦f(x,t) is measurable on E and integrable over E,
2.
for every t in I, the function
x↦∂f ∂t (x,t)

is measurable on E, and there exists a measurable set Z ⊂ E with μ(E ∖ Z) = 0 such that, for every x in Z, the map

t↦f(x,t)

is differentiable on I,

3.
there exists a function g: E → [0,∞), integrable over E, such that
|∂f ∂t (x,t) | ≤ g(x)

for every x in Z and every t in I.

Then the map

F(t) = ∫ Ef(x,t)dμ(x)

is differentiable on I and

F′(t) = ∫ E∂f ∂t (x,t)dμ(x)

for every t in I.

Proof. We apply Theorem 9.3 at each point t0 in I. □

Example 9.5 (Gamma function). For y > 0, we define

Γ(y) := ∫ 0∞e−xxy−1dx.

Then Γ is well defined and of class C∞ on ]0,∞[. More precisely, for every integer k ≥ 1 and every y > 0,

Γ(k)(y) = ∫ 0∞e−xxy−1(log ⁡x)kdx.

Proof. Step 1. Well-definedness. Let us first prove that Γ is well defined. Fix y > 0. Splitting the integral at 1, we obtain

∫ 0∞e−xxy−1dx = ∫ 01e−xxy−1dx + ∫ 1∞e−xxy−1dx.

On ]0, 1], we have e−x ≤ 1, hence

0 ≤ e−xxy−1 ≤ xy−1,

and the function x↦xy−1 is integrable on ]0, 1] because y > 0. On [1,∞[, the factor e−x gives exponential decay. More explicitly, there exists Cy > 0 such that xy−1 ≤ C yex∕2 for all x ≥ 1, and therefore

0 ≤ e−xxy−1 ≤ C ye−x∕2

for all x ≥ 1. Thus x↦e−xxy−1 is integrable on [1,∞[. Therefore Γ(y) is well defined for every y > 0.

Step 2. Uniform dominators on compact parameter intervals. We now prove continuity and smoothness on an arbitrary compact interval

J = [a,b] ⊂]0,∞[.

For every integer r ≥ 0, every y in J, and every x > 0, set

Fr(x,y) := e−xxy−1(log ⁡x)r.

For r = 0, this is the original integrand. If 0 < x ≤ 1, then a ≤ y and therefore

|Fr(x,y)|≤ xa−1| log ⁡x|r.

The function on the right is integrable on ]0, 1]. Indeed, by the classical one-dimensional change of variable x = e−t,

∫ 01xa−1| log ⁡x|rdx = ∫ 0∞e−attrdt < ∞.

If x ≥ 1, then y ≤ b and

|Fr(x,y)|≤ e−xxb−1(log ⁡x)r.

The function on the right is integrable on [1,∞[ because the exponential factor dominates the polynomial-logarithmic growth. Thus

gr(x) := 1]0,1](x)xa−1| log ⁡x|r + 1 [1,∞[(x)e−xxb−1(log ⁡x)r

is an integrable dominator for Fr(⋅,y), uniformly for y in J.

Step 3. Continuity of the candidate derivatives. For each fixed x > 0, the map y↦Fr(x,y) is continuous on J. Theorem 9.1 applied to F0 gives the continuity of Γ on J. More generally, for every r ≥ 0, Theorem 9.1 shows that the map

Gr(y) := ∫ 0∞F r(x,y)dx

is continuous on J.

Step 4. Induction on the order of differentiation. We now prove by induction that Γ is of class Ck on the interior of J and that Γ(k) = G k there for every k ≥ 0. The case k = 0 has just been proved. Assume that Γ is of class Ck on the interior of J and that Γ(k) = G k. Since, for every x > 0,

∂Fk ∂y (x,y) = Fk+1(x,y),

and since gk+1 is an integrable dominator for Fk+1 on J, Theorem 9.3 applies at each point y in the interior of J, after choosing a smaller open interval around y contained in the interior of J. Hence

Gk′(y) = ∫ 0∞F k+1(x,y)dx = Gk+1(y)

for every y in the interior of J. Since Gk+1 is continuous on J, this proves that Γ is of class Ck+1 on the interior of J and that Γ(k+1) = G k+1 there. Hence, for every k ≥ 1 and every y in the interior of J,

Γ(k)(y) = ∫ 0∞e−xxy−1(log ⁡x)kdx.

Since every point of ]0,∞[ lies in the interior of some such compact interval J, the function Γ is of class C∞ on ]0,∞[ and the displayed formula holds for all y > 0. □
