Integration and measure theory

Franck Sueur · Bachelor in Mathematics · University of Luxembourg

Start with σ-algebras
About these notes

We develop measure and integration from the abstract framework of measurable spaces and measures and then move toward the classical Lebesgue theory on ℝN. After introducing σ-algebras, Borel sets, and measures, we study measurable functions, simple functions, positive integration, and the main convergence theorems. We then turn to several fundamental applications: integrals with parameters, product measures, the Tonelli and Fubini theorems, modes of convergence, absolute continuity of measures and the Radon–Nikodym theorem, and finally the fundamental theorem of calculus together with one-dimensional and multidimensional change-of-variables formulas. In the Euclidean setting we discuss Lebesgue measure from two complementary viewpoints: the classical Carathéodory construction from rectangles and, later on, a construction through Radon measures and the Riesz representation theorem.

The notes also develop the structural side of measure theory. We introduce signed measures, Hahn and Jordan decompositions, total variation, absolute continuity and singularity, Lebesgue decomposition, and the Radon–Nikodym theorem. We then study positive and bounded Radon measures, the Riesz representation theorem, Lusin’s theorem, weak convergence of measures, and the Lebesgue spaces Lp(Ω,μ). This includes density results, convolution and mollifiers in the Euclidean setting, duality, weak topologies, and weak compactness statements. The aim is to provide a first coherent course in measure theory at bachelor level, while already preparing later developments in probability, partial differential equations, and functional analysis. We refer to the classical textbooks [5, 2, 7, 6, 3, 1] for complementary expositions.
