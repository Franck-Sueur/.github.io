8.1 The Lax–Milgram theorem and Stampacchia’s extension

Exercise 1.1 — Abstract Galerkin method and Céa’s lemma

Let un∈Vnu_{n}\in V_{n} be defined by

a⁢(un,vn)=⟨φ,vn⟩H′,H∀vn∈Vn.a(u_{n},v_{n})=\left\langle\varphi,v_{n}\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad% \forall v_{n}\in V_{n}.

Since VnV_{n} is finite-dimensional, it is a Hilbert space for the scalar product inherited from HH. The restriction of aa to Vn×VnV_{n}\times V_{n} is still continuous and coercive, so Lax–Milgram on VnV_{n} gives existence and uniqueness of unu_{n}.

Now let u∈Hu\in H be the exact solution. Subtracting the equations for uu and unu_{n}, we obtain the Galerkin orthogonality

a⁢(u−un,vn)=0∀vn∈Vn.a(u-u_{n},v_{n})=0\qquad\forall v_{n}\in V_{n}.

Fix any vn∈Vnv_{n}\in V_{n}. Since vn−un∈Vnv_{n}-u_{n}\in V_{n}, orthogonality gives

a⁢(u−un,vn−un)=0.a(u-u_{n},v_{n}-u_{n})=0.

Hence

a⁢(u−un,u−un)=a⁢(u−un,u−vn).a(u-u_{n},u-u_{n})=a(u-u_{n},u-v_{n}).

Using coercivity and continuity,

α⁢‖u−un‖H2≤a⁢(u−un,u−un)≤M⁢‖u−un‖H⁢‖u−vn‖H.\alpha\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq a(u-u_{n},u-u_{n})\leq M\left\|u-u_{n% }\right\|_{H}\,\left\|u-v_{n}\right\|_{H}.

If u≠unu\neq u_{n}, divide by ‖u−un‖H\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|_{H}; otherwise there is nothing to prove. This yields

‖u−un‖H≤Mα⁢‖u−vn‖H∀vn∈Vn.\left\|u-u_{n}\right\|_{H}\leq\frac{M}{\alpha}\left\|u-v_{n}\right\|_{H}\qquad% \forall v_{n}\in V_{n}.

Taking the infimum over vn∈Vnv_{n}\in V_{n} proves Céa’s estimate.

□\square

Exercise 1.2 — Affine constraints

Let K=u0+MK=u_{0}+M, with M⊂HM\subset H a closed linear subspace. Write any u∈Ku\in K as u=u0+mu=u_{0}+m with m∈Mm\in M. Likewise, every v∈Kv\in K can be written as v=u0+wv=u_{0}+w with w∈Mw\in M, so

v−u=w−m∈M.v-u=w-m\in M.

The Stampacchia variational inequality

a⁢(u,v−u)≥⟨φ,v−u⟩H′,H∀v∈Ka(u,v-u)\geq\left\langle\varphi,v-u\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall v\in K

is therefore equivalent to

a⁢(u,z)≥⟨φ,z⟩H′,H∀z∈M.a(u,z)\geq\left\langle\varphi,z\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall z\in M.

Because MM is a linear subspace, z∈Mz\in M implies −z∈M-z\in M. Applying the same inequality to zz and to −z-z forces equality:

a⁢(u,z)=⟨φ,z⟩H′,H∀z∈M.a(u,z)=\left\langle\varphi,z\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall z\in M.

Now write u=u0+mu=u_{0}+m. Then

a⁢(m,z)=⟨φ,z⟩H′,H−a⁢(u0,z)∀z∈M.a(m,z)=\left\langle\varphi,z\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}-a(u_{0},z)\qquad% \forall z\in M.

This is a Lax–Milgram problem on the Hilbert space MM, with unknown m∈Mm\in M. Since the restricted form is still continuous and coercive, there is a unique solution mm, hence a unique u=u0+m∈Ku=u_{0}+m\in K.

□\square

Exercise 1.3 — Stability with respect to the right-hand side

For i=1,2i=1,2, let ui∈Ku_{i}\in K solve the variational inequality with right-hand side φi\varphi_{i}. Use v=u2v=u_{2} in the inequality for u1u_{1}:

a⁢(u1,u2−u1)≥⟨φ1,u2−u1⟩H′,H.a(u_{1},u_{2}-u_{1})\geq\left\langle\varphi_{1},u_{2}-u_{1}\right\rangle_{H^{% \prime},H}.

Use v=u1v=u_{1} in the inequality for u2u_{2}:

a⁢(u2,u1−u2)≥⟨φ2,u1−u2⟩H′,H.a(u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})\geq\left\langle\varphi_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}\right\rangle_{H^{% \prime},H}.

Adding these two relations gives

a⁢(u1−u2,u1−u2)≤⟨φ1−φ2,u1−u2⟩H′,H.a(u_{1}-u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})\leq\left\langle\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2},u_{1}-u_{2}% \right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}.

By coercivity and by the definition of the dual norm,

α⁢‖u1−u2‖H2≤‖φ1−φ2‖H′⁢‖u1−u2‖H.\alpha\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}% \right\|_{H^{\prime}}\,\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{H}.

If u1≠u2u_{1}\neq u_{2}, divide by ‖u1−u2‖H\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{H}; otherwise the estimate is trivial. Hence

‖u1−u2‖H≤1α⁢‖φ1−φ2‖H′.\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{H}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{% 2}\right\|_{H^{\prime}}.

□\square

Exercise 1.4 — What can fail without coercivity

Every continuous linear functional on ℝ2\mathbb{R}^{2} has the form

φ⁢(y1,y2)=b1⁢y1+b2⁢y2.\varphi(y_{1},y_{2})=b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}.

We look for u=(x1,x2)∈ℝ2u=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2} such that

a⁢(u,v)=φ⁢(v)∀v=(y1,y2)∈ℝ2.a(u,v)=\varphi(v)\qquad\forall v=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Since

a⁢(u,v)=x1⁢y1,a(u,v)=x_{1}y_{1},

this means

x1⁢y1=b1⁢y1+b2⁢y2∀(y1,y2)∈ℝ2.x_{1}y_{1}=b_{1}y_{1}+b_{2}y_{2}\qquad\forall(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

This can hold only if b2=0b_{2}=0, because the left-hand side does not depend on y2y_{2}. When b2=0b_{2}=0, we must have x1=b1x_{1}=b_{1}, while x2x_{2} remains arbitrary.

Therefore:

  • •

    if φ⁢(y1,y2)=y2\varphi(y_{1},y_{2})=y_{2}, then b2=1b_{2}=1, so there is no solution;

  • •

    if φ⁢(y1,y2)=y1\varphi(y_{1},y_{2})=y_{1}, then b1=1b_{1}=1 and b2=0b_{2}=0, so every vector of the form (1,x2)(1,x_{2}) is a solution.

Thus, without coercivity, existence may fail and uniqueness may fail as well.

□\square

Exercise 1.5 — A diagonal example in ℓ2\ell^{2}

We seek u=(un)∈ℓ2u=(u_{n})\in\ell^{2} such that

∑n≥1λn⁢un⁢vn=∑n≥1bn⁢vn∀v=(vn)∈ℓ2.\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{n}u_{n}v_{n}=\sum_{n\geq 1}b_{n}v_{n}\qquad\forall v=(v% _{n})\in\ell^{2}.

Choose for vv the sequence supported at a single coordinate nn. Then

λn⁢un=bn∀n≥1.\lambda_{n}u_{n}=b_{n}\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

Hence the only possible solution is

un=bnλn∀n≥1.u_{n}=\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\qquad\forall n\geq 1.

Because λn≥α>0\lambda_{n}\geq\alpha>0, we have

∑n≥1|un|2≤1α2⁢∑n≥1|bn|2<∞.\sum_{n\geq 1}|u_{n}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}|b_{n}|^{2}<\infty.

So u∈ℓ2u\in\ell^{2}, and the Lax–Milgram solution is exactly

u=(bnλn)n≥1.u=\left(\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right)_{n\geq 1}.

□\square

Exercise 1.6 — The same diagonal example with a positivity constraint

We now solve the variational inequality on

K+={u=(un)∈ℓ2:un≥0⁢∀n}.K_{+}=\{u=(u_{n})\in\ell^{2}:u_{n}\geq 0\ \forall n\}.

It reads

∑n≥1(λn⁢un−bn)⁢(vn−un)≥0∀v∈K+.\sum_{n\geq 1}(\lambda_{n}u_{n}-b_{n})(v_{n}-u_{n})\geq 0\qquad\forall v\in K_% {+}.

Fix a coordinate nn, and choose v∈K+v\in K_{+} equal to uu on every coordinate except the nn-th one, where we set vn=t≥0v_{n}=t\geq 0. Then

(λn⁢un−bn)⁢(t−un)≥0∀t≥0.(\lambda_{n}u_{n}-b_{n})(t-u_{n})\geq 0\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

This is a one-dimensional variational inequality on the half-line. It implies

un=max⁡{bnλn,0}.u_{n}=\max\!\left\{\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}},0\right\}.

Indeed, if bn≤0b_{n}\leq 0, the only admissible value is un=0u_{n}=0; if bn>0b_{n}>0, then the unconstrained minimizer bn/λnb_{n}/\lambda_{n} is already nonnegative, so it is the solution.

Moreover,

0≤un≤|bnλn|,0\leq u_{n}\leq\left|\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|,

so u∈ℓ2u\in\ell^{2}. Therefore the constrained solution is the coordinatewise positive part of the unconstrained one.

□\square

Exercise 1.7 — A box constraint in ℓ2\ell^{2}

We now impose the box constraint

K[0,1]={u=(un)∈ℓ2:0≤un≤1⁢∀n}.K_{[0,1]}=\{u=(u_{n})\in\ell^{2}:0\leq u_{n}\leq 1\ \forall n\}.

The variational inequality becomes

∑n≥1(λn⁢un−bn)⁢(vn−un)≥0∀v∈K[0,1].\sum_{n\geq 1}(\lambda_{n}u_{n}-b_{n})(v_{n}-u_{n})\geq 0\qquad\forall v\in K_% {[0,1]}.

As in Exercise 1.6, fix nn and vary only the nn-th coordinate. For every t∈[0,1]t\in[0,1], we obtain

(λn⁢un−bn)⁢(t−un)≥0∀t∈[0,1].(\lambda_{n}u_{n}-b_{n})(t-u_{n})\geq 0\qquad\forall t\in[0,1].

This scalar variational inequality means exactly that unu_{n} is the projection of bn/λnb_{n}/\lambda_{n} onto the interval [0,1][0,1]:

un=Π[0,1]⁢(bnλn):=min⁡{1,max⁡{0,bnλn}}.u_{n}=\Pi_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right):=\min\!\left\{1,\max% \!\left\{0,\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right\}\right\}.

As before,

0≤un≤|bnλn|,0\leq u_{n}\leq\left|\frac{b_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}\right|,

so u∈ℓ2u\in\ell^{2}. Hence the solution is obtained by coordinatewise truncation of the unconstrained solution.

□\square

Part 2. Finite element space, nodal basis, and nodal interpolation

  1. (a)

    On each rectangle KK, the restriction vh|Kv_{h}|_{K} is a polynomial in Q1Q_{1}, hence it belongs to H1⁢(K)H^{1}(K).   2. (b)

    |u⁢(x,t)|≤‖u0‖L∞⁢(ℝ)+∫0t‖f⁢(s)‖L∞⁢(ℝ)⁢𝑑s.\left|u(x,t)\right|\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^% {t}\left\|f(s)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\,ds. (8.258)

    Taking the supremum with respect to xx gives the announced estimate.

□\square

Exercise 7.2 — Inflow transport on a finite interval

  1. (a)

    Let (x,t)(x,t) be fixed and consider the characteristic passing through this point. It is given by

    X⁢(s)=x−a⁢(t−s)=x−a⁢t+a⁢s.X(s)=x-a(t-s)=x-at+as. (8.259)

    Along this curve we have

    dd⁢s⁢u⁢(X⁢(s),s)=∂tu⁢(X⁢(s),s)+a⁢∂xu⁢(X⁢(s),s)=0,\frac{d}{ds}u(X(s),s)=\partial_{t}u(X(s),s)+a\,\partial_{x}u(X(s),s)=0, (8.260)

    so uu is constant along the characteristic.

    If x−a⁢t>αx-at>\alpha, the backward characteristic reaches the initial line at the point (x−a⁢t,0)(x-at,0) before touching the boundary. Hence

    u⁢(x,t)=u0⁢(x−a⁢t).u(x,t)=u_{0}(x-at). (8.261)

    If x−a⁢t≤αx-at\leq\alpha, the backward characteristic reaches the inflow boundary x=αx=\alpha at the time

    s∗=t−x−αa≥0.s_{*}=t-\frac{x-\alpha}{a}\geq 0. (8.262)

    Therefore

    u⁢(x,t)=u⁢(α,s∗)=ϕ⁢(t−x−αa).u(x,t)=u(\alpha,s_{*})=\phi\!\left(t-\frac{x-\alpha}{a}\right). (8.263)

    This proves the announced formula.

  2. (b)

    The explicit formula of part (a) shows that every value u⁢(x,t)u(x,t) is obtained either from the initial datum or from the inflow boundary datum. Therefore

    |u⁢(x,t)|≤max⁡{‖u0‖L∞⁢(α,β),‖ϕ‖L∞⁢(0,t)}for all ⁢x∈(α,β).\left|u(x,t)\right|\leq\max\!\left\{\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\alpha,% \beta)},\,\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}\right\}\qquad\text{for all }x% \in(\alpha,\beta). (8.264)

    Taking the supremum with respect to xx yields

    ‖u⁢(t)‖L∞⁢(α,β)≤max⁡{‖u0‖L∞⁢(α,β),‖ϕ‖L∞⁢(0,t)}.\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\alpha,\beta)}\leq\max\!\left\{\left\|u_{0}% \right\|_{L^{\infty}(\alpha,\beta)},\,\left\|\phi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,t)}% \right\}. (8.265)
  3. (c)

    We multiply the equation by uu and integrate over (α,β)(\alpha,\beta):

    ∫αβ∂tu⁢u⁢d⁢x+a⁢∫αβ∂xu⁢u⁢d⁢x=0.\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{t}u\,u\,dx+a\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{x}u% \,u\,dx=0. (8.266)

    As in the energy estimate derived in Chapter 7,

    ∫αβ∂tu⁢u⁢d⁢x=12⁢dd⁢t⁢‖u⁢(t)‖L2⁢(α,β)2,\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{t}u\,u\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u(t)% \right\|_{L^{2}(\alpha,\beta)}^{2}, (8.267)

    and

    a⁢∫αβ∂xu⁢u⁢d⁢x=a2⁢|u⁢(β,t)|2−a2⁢|u⁢(α,t)|2=a2⁢|u⁢(β,t)|2−a2⁢|ϕ⁢(t)|2.a\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\partial_{x}u\,u\,dx=\frac{a}{2}\left|u(\beta,t)\right|^% {2}-\frac{a}{2}\left|u(\alpha,t)\right|^{2}=\frac{a}{2}\left|u(\beta,t)\right|% ^{2}-\frac{a}{2}\left|\phi(t)\right|^{2}. (8.268)

    Thus

    12⁢dd⁢t⁢‖u⁢(t)‖L2⁢(α,β)2+a2⁢|u⁢(β,t)|2=a2⁢|ϕ⁢(t)|2.\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\alpha,\beta)}^{2}+\frac{a}{% 2}\left|u(\beta,t)\right|^{2}=\frac{a}{2}\left|\phi(t)\right|^{2}. (8.269)

    Integrating from 0 to tt gives

    ‖u⁢(t)‖L2⁢(α,β)2+a⁢∫0t|u⁢(β,s)|2⁢𝑑s=‖u0‖L2⁢(α,β)2+a⁢∫0t|ϕ⁢(s)|2⁢𝑑s.\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\alpha,\beta)}^{2}+a\int_{0}^{t}\left|u(\beta,s)% \right|^{2}\,ds=\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\alpha,\beta)}^{2}+a\int_{0}^{t}% \left|\phi(s)\right|^{2}\,ds. (8.270)

    ‖u⁢(t)‖L2⁢(α,β)2≤‖u0‖L2⁢(α,β)2+a⁢∫0t|ϕ⁢(s)|2⁢𝑑s.\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\alpha,\beta)}^{2}\leq\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(% \alpha,\beta)}^{2}+a\int_{0}^{t}\left|\phi(s)\right|^{2}\,ds. (8.271)

Exercise 7.3 — Fourier analysis of the classical schemes

  1. (a)

    We insert the Fourier mode

    ujn=u^n⁢ei⁢j⁢θu_{j}^{n}=\widehat{u}^{\,n}e^{ij\theta} (8.272)

    into each scheme.

    For FTCS,

    u^n+1⁢ei⁢j⁢θ\displaystyle\widehat{u}^{\,n+1}e^{ij\theta} =u^n⁢ei⁢j⁢θ−ν2⁢u^n⁢(ei⁢(j+1)⁢θ−ei⁢(j−1)⁢θ)\displaystyle=\widehat{u}^{\,n}e^{ij\theta}-\frac{\nu}{2}\widehat{u}^{\,n}(e^{% i(j+1)\theta}-e^{i(j-1)\theta}) (8.273)
    =(1−ν2⁢(ei⁢θ−e−i⁢θ))⁢u^n⁢ei⁢j⁢θ,\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\nu}{2}(e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta})\right)\widehat{u% }^{\,n}e^{ij\theta}, (8.274)

    gFTCS⁢(θ)=1−i⁢ν⁢sin⁡θ.g_{\mathrm{FTCS}}(\theta)=1-i\nu\sin\theta. (8.275)

    For the upwind scheme,

    gU⁢(θ)=1−ν⁢(1−e−i⁢θ).g_{\mathrm{U}}(\theta)=1-\nu(1-e^{-i\theta}). (8.276)

    For Lax–Friedrichs,

    gLF⁢(θ)=12⁢(ei⁢θ+e−i⁢θ)−ν2⁢(ei⁢θ−e−i⁢θ)=cos⁡θ−i⁢ν⁢sin⁡θ.g_{\mathrm{LF}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})-\frac{\nu}{2}(e^% {i\theta}-e^{-i\theta})=\cos\theta-i\nu\sin\theta. (8.277)

    For Lax–Wendroff,

    gLW⁢(θ)\displaystyle g_{\mathrm{LW}}(\theta) =1−ν2⁢(ei⁢θ−e−i⁢θ)+ν22⁢(ei⁢θ−2+e−i⁢θ)\displaystyle=1-\frac{\nu}{2}(e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta})+\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}(e^{i% \theta}-2+e^{-i\theta}) (8.278)
    =1−i⁢ν⁢sin⁡θ+ν2⁢(cos⁡θ−1).\displaystyle=1-i\nu\sin\theta+\nu^{2}(\cos\theta-1). (8.279)
  2. (b)

    For FTCS,

    |gFTCS⁢(θ)|2=1+ν2⁢sin2⁡θ.\left|g_{\mathrm{FTCS}}(\theta)\right|^{2}=1+\nu^{2}\sin^{2}\theta. (8.280)

    If ν≠0\nu\neq 0 and sin⁡θ≠0\sin\theta\neq 0, then

    |gFTCS⁢(θ)|>1.\left|g_{\mathrm{FTCS}}(\theta)\right|>1. (8.281)

    Therefore the amplification factor is not bounded by 11 on [−π,π][-\pi,\pi], so the scheme cannot be strongly stable in ℓh2\ell_{h}^{2}.

    Under the hyperbolic scaling, ν\nu is fixed. On arbitrarily fine periodic grids, choosing a mode with sin2⁡θ≥1/2\sin^{2}\theta\geq 1/2 gives

    |gFTCS⁢(θ)|≥1+ν22>1.\left|g_{\mathrm{FTCS}}(\theta)\right|\geq\sqrt{1+\frac{\nu^{2}}{2}}>1. (8.282)

    After nn steps, this mode is multiplied by |gFTCS⁢(θ)|n\left|g_{\mathrm{FTCS}}(\theta)\right|^{n}. Since nn is of order T/Δ⁢tT/\Delta t on a fixed time interval, this amplification is unbounded as Δ⁢t→0\Delta t\to 0. Hence FTCS is not stable in ℓh2\ell_{h}^{2} on finite time intervals under hyperbolic scaling. This conclusion is obtained by this explicit Fourier-mode construction, not by invoking a converse of Theorem 7.20.

  3. (c)

    We compute the moduli of the stable schemes. For upwind,

    |gU⁢(θ)|2\displaystyle\left|g_{\mathrm{U}}(\theta)\right|^{2} =|1−ν+ν⁢e−i⁢θ|2\displaystyle=\left|1-\nu+\nu e^{-i\theta}\right|^{2} (8.283)
    =(1−ν+ν⁢cos⁡θ)2+ν2⁢sin2⁡θ\displaystyle=(1-\nu+\nu\cos\theta)^{2}+\nu^{2}\sin^{2}\theta (8.284)
    =1−2⁢ν⁢(1−ν)⁢(1−cos⁡θ)≤1.\displaystyle=1-2\nu(1-\nu)(1-\cos\theta)\leq 1. (8.285)

    For Lax–Friedrichs,

    |gLF⁢(θ)|2=cos2⁡θ+ν2⁢sin2⁡θ=1−(1−ν2)⁢sin2⁡θ≤1,\left|g_{\mathrm{LF}}(\theta)\right|^{2}=\cos^{2}\theta+\nu^{2}\sin^{2}\theta=% 1-(1-\nu^{2})\sin^{2}\theta\leq 1, (8.286)

    because 0≤ν≤10\leq\nu\leq 1. For Lax–Wendroff, we write

    gLW⁢(θ)=1−ν2⁢(1−cos⁡θ)−i⁢ν⁢sin⁡θ.g_{\mathrm{LW}}(\theta)=1-\nu^{2}(1-\cos\theta)-i\nu\sin\theta. (8.287)

    Hence

    |gLW⁢(θ)|2\displaystyle\left|g_{\mathrm{LW}}(\theta)\right|^{2} =(1−ν2⁢(1−cos⁡θ))2+ν2⁢sin2⁡θ\displaystyle=\left(1-\nu^{2}(1-\cos\theta)\right)^{2}+\nu^{2}\sin^{2}\theta (8.288)

    The von Neumann criterion stated in Theorem 7.20 then yields strong ℓh2\ell_{h}^{2} stability of upwind, Lax–Friedrichs, and Lax–Wendroff on the periodic grid.

  4. (d)

    The upwind scheme can be rewritten as

    ujn+1=(1−ν)⁢ujn+ν⁢uj−1n.u_{j}^{n+1}=(1-\nu)u_{j}^{n}+\nu u_{j-1}^{n}. (8.290)

    When 0≤ν≤10\leq\nu\leq 1, the coefficients form a convex combination. Hence

    |ujn+1|≤(1−ν)⁢supk|ukn|+ν⁢supk|ukn|=supk|ukn|.\left|u_{j}^{n+1}\right|\leq(1-\nu)\sup_{k}\left|u_{k}^{n}\right|+\nu\sup_{k}% \left|u_{k}^{n}\right|=\sup_{k}\left|u_{k}^{n}\right|. (8.291)

Exercise 7.4 — The wave equation as a first-order hyperbolic system

  1. (a)

    The characteristic polynomial of AA is

    det(A−μ⁢I)=μ2−γ2,\det(A-\mu I)=\mu^{2}-\gamma^{2}, (8.293)

    so the eigenvalues are μ=γ\mu=\gamma and μ=−γ\mu=-\gamma.

    For μ=γ\mu=\gamma, we solve

    (−γ−γ−γ−γ)⁢(xy)=0,\begin{pmatrix}-\gamma&-\gamma\\ -\gamma&-\gamma\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\ y\end{pmatrix}=0, (8.294)

    which gives y=−xy=-x. We may choose

    r+=(1−1).r_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\ -1\end{pmatrix}. (8.295)

    For μ=−γ\mu=-\gamma, we similarly obtain

    r−=(11).r_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}. (8.296)

    Therefore we may take

    T=(11−11),Λ=(γ00−γ).T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\ -1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&0\\ 0&-\gamma\end{pmatrix}. (8.297)

    Since detT=2\det T=2, we have

    T−1=12⁢(1−111).T^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\ 1&1\end{pmatrix}. (8.298)

    Hence A=T⁢Λ⁢T−1A=T\Lambda T^{-1}.

  2. (b)

    Since U=T⁢WU=TW, the system

    ∂tU+A⁢∂xU=0\partial_{t}U+A\,\partial_{x}U=0 (8.299)

    becomes

    ∂tW+Λ⁢∂xW=0.\partial_{t}W+\Lambda\,\partial_{x}W=0. (8.300)

    ∂tw++γ⁢∂xw+\displaystyle\partial_{t}w_{+}+\gamma\,\partial_{x}w_{+} =0,\displaystyle=0, (8.301)

    Thus w+w_{+} is transported to the right with velocity γ\gamma, whereas w−w_{-} is transported to the left with velocity −γ-\gamma.

    Moreover,

    W=T−1⁢U=12⁢(v−qv+q)=12⁢(∂tu−γ⁢∂xu∂tu+γ⁢∂xu).W=T^{-1}U=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}v-q\\ v+q\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{t}u-\gamma\partial_{x}u\\ \partial_{t}u+\gamma\partial_{x}u\end{pmatrix}. (8.303)
  3. (c)

    Applying the scalar Lax–Wendroff scheme componentwise to WW gives

    Wjn+1=Wjn−Δ⁢t2⁢h⁢Λ⁢(Wj+1n−Wj−1n)+Δ⁢t22⁢h2⁢Λ2⁢(Wj+1n−2⁢Wjn+Wj−1n).W_{j}^{n+1}=W_{j}^{n}-\frac{\Delta t}{2h}\Lambda(W_{j+1}^{n}-W_{j-1}^{n})+% \frac{\Delta t^{2}}{2h^{2}}\Lambda^{2}(W_{j+1}^{n}-2W_{j}^{n}+W_{j-1}^{n}). (8.304)

    Ujn+1\displaystyle U_{j}^{n+1} =Ujn−Δ⁢t2⁢h⁢T⁢Λ⁢T−1⁢(Uj+1n−Uj−1n)\displaystyle=U_{j}^{n}-\frac{\Delta t}{2h}T\Lambda T^{-1}(U_{j+1}^{n}-U_{j-1}% ^{n}) (8.305)

    Ujn+1=Ujn−Δ⁢t2⁢h⁢A⁢(Uj+1n−Uj−1n)+Δ⁢t22⁢h2⁢A2⁢(Uj+1n−2⁢Ujn+Uj−1n).U_{j}^{n+1}=U_{j}^{n}-\frac{\Delta t}{2h}A(U_{j+1}^{n}-U_{j-1}^{n})+\frac{% \Delta t^{2}}{2h^{2}}A^{2}(U_{j+1}^{n}-2U_{j}^{n}+U_{j-1}^{n}). (8.307)
