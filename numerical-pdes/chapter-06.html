Chapter 6 The Lax–Richtmyer theorem

The finite element chapters converted approximation estimates into error bounds using coercivity and Céa’s lemma. We now examine consistency, stability, and convergence before applying these ideas to time-dependent finite difference schemes in Chapter 7. We first prove a simple fixed-space operator version. We then give an evolution error estimate and a Lax–Richtmyer equivalence theorem that explicitly connects continuous data with changing grid spaces.

6.1 Operator equations and discretizations

We model a general situation in the numerical solution of PDEs by an operator equation of the form

L⁢x=y,Lx=y, (6.1)

where yy is given in YY, and the unknown is xx in XX, where XX and YY are two Banach spaces.

Definition 6.1.

Let XX and YY be Banach spaces and let LL be in ℒ⁢(X,Y)\mathcal{L}(X,Y), that is a bounded linear operator from XX to YY. We say that LL is well posed if for each yy in YY there exists a unique xx in XX solving (6.1).

Equivalently, LL is bijective and, by the open mapping theorem recalled in Section 6.3, L−1L^{-1} belongs to ℒ⁢(Y,X)\mathcal{L}(Y,X).

Throughout this section, assume that L∈ℒ⁢(X,Y)L\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y) is bijective. Let H⊂(0,+∞)H\subset(0,+\infty) be a set of admissible discretization parameters with 0 as an accumulation point. All limits h→0h\to 0 are taken within HH. We consider a family (Lh)h∈H(L_{h})_{h\in H} in ℒ⁢(X,Y)\mathcal{L}(X,Y) and, for each hh, the discrete problem

Lh⁢xh=y.L_{h}x_{h}=y. (6.2)

We assume (6.2) is well posed for every hh in HH, so that LhL_{h} is bijective and Lh−1L_{h}^{-1} belongs to ℒ⁢(Y,X)\mathcal{L}(Y,X).

Definition 6.2.

The discretization (Lh)h∈H(L_{h})_{h\in H} is:

  • •

    stable if there exist constants C>0C>0 and h0>0h_{0}>0 such that

    ‖Lh−1‖Y→X≤Cfor all h∈H with h<h0.\left\|L_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{Y\to X}\leq C\qquad\text{for all $h\in H$ with $h<h% _{0}$.} (6.3)
  • •

    consistent if for every xx in XX,

    ‖L⁢x−Lh⁢x‖Y→0as h→0.\left\|Lx-L_{h}x\right\|_{Y}\to 0\qquad\text{as $h\to 0$.} (6.4)
  • •

    convergent if for every yy in YY, letting x:=L−1⁢yx:=L^{-1}y solve (6.1) and xh:=Lh−1⁢yx_{h}:=L_{h}^{-1}y solve (6.2), we have

    ‖x−xh‖X→0as h→0.\left\|x-x_{h}\right\|_{X}\to 0\qquad\text{as $h\to 0$.} (6.5)

6.2 The fixed-space operator version

Theorem 6.3.

Let X,YX,Y be Banach spaces, and assume that LL and every LhL_{h} are bounded linear bijections from XX to YY. Assume that (Lh)h∈H(L_{h})_{h\in H} is consistent, i.e. (6.4) holds for all xx in XX. Then stability (6.3) is equivalent to convergence (6.5).

Proof.

Let yy be fixed in YY, and set x:=L−1⁢yx:=L^{-1}y and xh:=Lh−1⁢yx_{h}:=L_{h}^{-1}y. We use Lh⁢xh=y=L⁢xL_{h}x_{h}=y=Lx to write

x−xh\displaystyle x-x_{h} =Lh−1⁢(Lh⁢x−Lh⁢xh)\displaystyle=L_{h}^{-1}(L_{h}x-L_{h}x_{h})
=Lh−1⁢(Lh⁢x−L⁢x).\displaystyle=L_{h}^{-1}(L_{h}x-Lx). (6.6)

Stability ⇒\Rightarrow convergence. Assume (6.3). Taking norms in (6.6) yields

‖x−xh‖X≤‖Lh−1‖Y→X⁢‖Lh⁢x−L⁢x‖Y≤C⁢‖Lh⁢x−L⁢x‖Y.\left\|x-x_{h}\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|L_{h}^{-1}\right\|_{Y\to X}\,\left\|L_{h}% x-Lx\right\|_{Y}\leq C\,\left\|L_{h}x-Lx\right\|_{Y}. (6.7)

By consistency, the right-hand side tends to 0 as h→0h\to 0, hence (6.5) holds.

Convergence ⇒\Rightarrow stability. Assume (6.5) holds for every yy in YY. Equivalently, for every yy in YY we have

Lh−1⁢y→L−1⁢yin X as h→0.L_{h}^{-1}y\to L^{-1}y\qquad\text{in $X$ as $h\to 0$.} (6.8)

Suppose, for a contradiction, that no uniform inverse bound holds for sufficiently small hh. We can then choose hm∈Hh_{m}\in H with hm<1/mh_{m}<1/m and ‖Lhm−1‖Y→X>m\left\|L_{h_{m}}^{-1}\right\|_{Y\to X}>m. For each fixed y∈Yy\in Y, the sequence Lhm−1⁢yL_{h_{m}}^{-1}y converges, and hence is bounded. The uniform boundedness principle recalled in Section 6.4, applied to this sequence, gives supm‖Lhm−1‖Y→X<+∞\sup_{m}\left\|L_{h_{m}}^{-1}\right\|_{Y\to X}<+\infty, a contradiction. Thus (6.3) holds for sufficiently small hh. ∎

Remark 6.4.

The theorem is often paraphrased as: for a consistent discretization, the only missing ingredient for convergence is stability (and conversely). A classical reference is [3, Theorem 1.1].

6.3 A reminder on the open mapping theorem

Theorem 6.5.

Let XX and YY be Banach spaces and let L∈ℒ⁢(X,Y)L\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y) be surjective. Then LL is an open map: if U⊂XU\subset X is open, then L⁢(U)⊂YL(U)\subset Y is open.

Corollary 6.6.

Let XX and YY be Banach spaces and let L∈ℒ⁢(X,Y)L\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y) be bijective. Then L−1:Y→XL^{-1}:Y\to X belongs to ℒ⁢(Y,X)\mathcal{L}(Y,X). Equivalently, there exists a constant C>0C>0 such that

‖x‖X≤C⁢‖L⁢x‖Yfor all x∈X.\left\|x\right\|_{X}\leq C\,\left\|Lx\right\|_{Y}\qquad\text{for all $x\in X$.}

6.4 A reminder on the uniform boundedness principle (Banach–Steinhaus)

Theorem 6.7.

Let XX be a Banach space, let II be a nonempty set, let YY be a normed space, and let (Ti)i∈I(T_{i})_{i\in I} be a family in ℒ⁢(X,Y)\mathcal{L}(X,Y). Assume that for every xx in XX the set {‖Ti⁢x‖Y:i∈I}\{\left\|T_{i}x\right\|_{Y}:i\in I\} is bounded, i.e.

supi‖Ti⁢x‖Y<+∞for all x in X.\sup_{i}\left\|T_{i}x\right\|_{Y}<+\infty\qquad\text{for all $x$ in $X$.} (6.9)

Then the operator norms are uniformly bounded:

supi‖Ti‖X→Y<+∞.\sup_{i}\left\|T_{i}\right\|_{X\to Y}<+\infty. (6.10)
Remark 6.8.

We recall that ‖T‖X→Y:=sup‖x‖X=1‖T⁢x‖Y\left\|T\right\|_{X\to Y}:=\sup_{\left\|x\right\|_{X}=1}\left\|Tx\right\|_{Y} for TT in ℒ⁢(X,Y)\mathcal{L}(X,Y). The statement is sometimes phrased as pointwise boundedness implies uniform boundedness.

The following corollary is tailored to discretization families.

Corollary 6.9.

Let XX be a Banach space, let YY be a normed space, and let (Th)h∈H(T_{h})_{h\in H} be a family in ℒ⁢(X,Y)\mathcal{L}(X,Y). Assume that for each xx in XX we have a limit Th⁢x→T⁢xT_{h}x\to Tx in YY as h→0h\to 0 for some map T:X→YT:X\to Y. Then there exists h0>0h_{0}>0 such that suph∈H,h<h0‖Th‖X→Y<+∞\sup_{h\in H,\,h<h_{0}}\left\|T_{h}\right\|_{X\to Y}<+\infty.

Proof.

If the conclusion failed, we could choose hm<1/mh_{m}<1/m with ‖Thm‖X→Y>m\left\|T_{h_{m}}\right\|_{X\to Y}>m. For each x∈Xx\in X, the convergent sequence Thm⁢xT_{h_{m}}x is bounded. The uniform boundedness principle applied to (Thm)(T_{h_{m}}) contradicts the choice of its operator norms. ∎

Remark 6.10.

Convergence as h→0h\to 0 says nothing about coarse parameters away from zero. For example, on H=(0,1)H=(0,1) the scalar operators L=IL=I and Lh=(1−h)⁢IL_{h}=(1-h)I are consistent and convergent, but suph∈H‖Lh−1‖=+∞\sup_{h\in H}\left\|L_{h}^{-1}\right\|=+\infty. This is why stability is defined for sufficiently small parameters. For a refinement sequence H={hm:m≥1}H=\{h_{m}:m\geq 1\} with hm→0h_{m}\to 0, adding its finitely many coarse operators does not affect boundedness.

6.5 Stability of time-discrete evolutions

Definition 6.11.

Let (Xh,∥⋅∥Xh)(X_{h},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X_{h}}) be a family of normed spaces, let Δ⁢t=Δ⁢t⁢(h)→0\Delta t=\Delta t(h)\to 0, and let Gh∈ℒ⁢(Xh,Xh)G_{h}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{h},X_{h}). Write the linear time-discrete evolution as

Un+1=Gh⁢Un,tn=n⁢Δ⁢t.U^{n+1}=G_{h}U^{n},\qquad t^{n}=n\Delta t. (6.11)

We say that this evolution is stable in XhX_{h} on finite time intervals if, for every T>0T>0, there exists a constant CT>0C_{T}>0, independent of hh, Δ⁢t\Delta t, and nn, such that, for all sufficiently small admissible hh,

‖Ghn⁢V‖Xh≤CT⁢‖V‖Xhfor all ⁢V∈Xh⁢ and all ⁢0≤n⁢Δ⁢t≤T.\left\|G_{h}^{n}V\right\|_{X_{h}}\leq C_{T}\left\|V\right\|_{X_{h}}\qquad\text% {for all }V\in X_{h}\text{ and all }0\leq n\Delta t\leq T. (6.12)

We say that the evolution is strongly stable in XhX_{h} if, for all sufficiently small admissible hh, its one-step update is non-expansive:

‖Gh⁢V‖Xh≤‖V‖Xhfor all ⁢V∈Xh.\left\|G_{h}V\right\|_{X_{h}}\leq\left\|V\right\|_{X_{h}}\qquad\text{for all }% V\in X_{h}. (6.13)

By iteration, strong stability implies (6.12) with CT=1C_{T}=1 on every finite time interval.

Remark 6.12.

Definition 6.11 expresses power boundedness for a time evolution. The fixed-space inverse-operator theorem does not itself establish equivalence for this setting; Theorem 6.15 below supplies the connection. The norm is part of the statement. In Chapter 7, for example, Xh=ℓh2X_{h}=\ell_{h}^{2} and stability means boundedness in the topology

ℓ∞⁢(0,T;ℓh2),max0≤n⁢Δ⁢t≤T⁡‖Un‖ℓh2.\ell^{\infty}(0,T;\ell_{h}^{2}),\qquad\max_{0\leq n\Delta t\leq T}\left\|U^{n}% \right\|_{\ell_{h}^{2}}. (6.14)

Strong stability is a non-expansion property in the chosen norm; it is stronger than stability on finite time intervals.

6.6 An error estimate for discrete evolutions

For a time-dependent equation, consistency is measured by inserting an exact trajectory into the update rule. The resulting defect must be divided by Δ⁢t\Delta t: on a finite time interval, there are of order 1/Δ⁢t1/\Delta t steps.

Theorem 6.13.

Fix T>0T>0 and let NT=⌊T/Δ⁢t⌋≥1N_{T}=\lfloor T/\Delta t\rfloor\geq 1. Assume that Gh∈ℒ⁢(Xh,Xh)G_{h}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{h},X_{h}) satisfies (6.12). Let Un+1=Gh⁢UnU^{n+1}=G_{h}U^{n}, and let (Whn)0≤n≤NT(W_{h}^{n})_{0\leq n\leq N_{T}} be reference values in XhX_{h}. Define the error and the normalized defect by

en=Un−Whn,τhn=Whn+1−Gh⁢WhnΔ⁢t(0≤n<NT).e^{n}=U^{n}-W_{h}^{n},\qquad\tau_{h}^{n}=\frac{W_{h}^{n+1}-G_{h}W_{h}^{n}}{% \Delta t}\quad(0\leq n<N_{T}). (6.15)

Then

max0≤n≤NT⁡‖en‖Xh\displaystyle\max_{0\leq n\leq N_{T}}\left\|e^{n}\right\|_{X_{h}} ≤CT⁢(‖e0‖Xh+Δ⁢t⁢∑j=0NT−1‖τhj‖Xh)\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\left(\left\|e^{0}\right\|_{X_{h}}+\Delta t\sum_{j=0}^{% N_{T}-1}\left\|\tau_{h}^{j}\right\|_{X_{h}}\right) (6.16)
≤CT⁢(‖e0‖Xh+T⁢max0≤j<NT⁡‖τhj‖Xh).\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\left(\left\|e^{0}\right\|_{X_{h}}+T\max_{0\leq j<N_{T}% }\left\|\tau_{h}^{j}\right\|_{X_{h}}\right).

In particular, an initial error tending to zero and a normalized defect tending to zero uniformly in time imply convergence to the reference trajectory. If both are O⁢(hp)O(h^{p}), the error is O⁢(hp)O(h^{p}) on [0,T][0,T].

Proof.

Subtracting the reference update gives

en+1=Gh⁢en−Δ⁢t⁢τhn.e^{n+1}=G_{h}e^{n}-\Delta t\,\tau_{h}^{n}. (6.17)

Iteration yields the discrete Duhamel formula

en=Ghn⁢e0−Δ⁢t⁢∑j=0n−1Ghn−1−j⁢τhj.e^{n}=G_{h}^{n}e^{0}-\Delta t\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}G_{h}^{n-1-j}\tau_{h}^{j}. (6.18)

Every power appearing here corresponds to a time at most TT. Taking norms and using stability proves (6.16). ∎

6.7 Lax–Richtmyer equivalence on changing grid spaces

The preceding estimate compares two trajectories on the same grid. To compare numerical solutions with arbitrary continuous data, we must also specify how data are projected onto the grid and how grid values are reconstructed as functions. This avoids using point values of an L2L^{2} equivalence class.

Let XX be a Banach space and let S⁢(t):X→XS(t):X\to X, t≥0t\geq 0, be a strongly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators: S⁢(0)=IS(0)=I, S⁢(t+s)=S⁢(t)⁢S⁢(s)S(t+s)=S(t)S(s), and t↦S⁢(t)⁢xt\mapsto S(t)x is continuous for every x∈Xx\in X. Assume sup0≤t≤T‖S⁢(t)‖X→X≤MT\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|S(t)\right\|_{X\to X}\leq M_{T} for each T>0T>0. For every admissible hh, let XhX_{h} be a normed grid space and let

Ph:X→Xh,Eh:Xh→XP_{h}:X\to X_{h},\qquad E_{h}:X_{h}\to X (6.19)

be bounded linear maps satisfying

‖Ph‖X→Xh≤1,‖Eh⁢V‖X=‖V‖Xhfor every ⁢V∈Xh,Ph⁢Eh=IXh,Eh⁢Ph⁢x⟶xfor every ⁢x∈X.\begin{gathered}\left\|P_{h}\right\|_{X\to X_{h}}\leq 1,\qquad\left\|E_{h}V% \right\|_{X}=\left\|V\right\|_{X_{h}}\quad\text{for every }V\in X_{h},\\ P_{h}E_{h}=I_{X_{h}},\qquad E_{h}P_{h}x\longrightarrow x\quad\text{for every }% x\in X.\end{gathered} (6.20)

These assumptions hold for cell averages and piecewise constant reconstruction in L2L^{2} on a periodic interval, as explained in Chapter 7. Let Δ⁢t=Δ⁢t⁢(h)→0\Delta t=\Delta t(h)\to 0 and Gh∈ℒ⁢(Xh,Xh)G_{h}\in\mathcal{L}(X_{h},X_{h}).

Definition 6.14.

The approximation is consistent on a dense class D⊂XD\subset X if, for every x∈Dx\in D and every T>0T>0,

max0≤n<NT⁡‖Ph⁢S⁢(tn+1)⁢x−Gh⁢Ph⁢S⁢(tn)⁢xΔ⁢t‖Xh⟶0,NT=⌊T/Δ⁢t⌋.\max_{0\leq n<N_{T}}\left\|\frac{P_{h}S(t^{n+1})x-G_{h}P_{h}S(t^{n})x}{\Delta t% }\right\|_{X_{h}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad N_{T}=\lfloor T/\Delta t\rfloor. (6.21)

It is convergent for all data in XX if, for every x∈Xx\in X and every T>0T>0,

max0≤n≤NT⁡‖Eh⁢Ghn⁢Ph⁢x−S⁢(tn)⁢x‖X⟶0.\max_{0\leq n\leq N_{T}}\left\|E_{h}G_{h}^{n}P_{h}x-S(t^{n})x\right\|_{X}% \longrightarrow 0. (6.22)

The initial grid vector in this definition is U0=Ph⁢xU^{0}=P_{h}x.

Theorem 6.15.

Under (6.20) and consistency (6.21) on a dense class D⊂XD\subset X, finite-time stability (6.12) is equivalent to convergence (6.22) for all data in XX. Both assertions concern sufficiently small admissible hh.

Proof.

Stability implies convergence. For x∈Dx\in D, apply Theorem 6.13 with Whn=Ph⁢S⁢(tn)⁢xW_{h}^{n}=P_{h}S(t^{n})x and U0=Ph⁢xU^{0}=P_{h}x. The initial error is zero and consistency makes the defect tend uniformly to zero. Since EhE_{h} is isometric, it remains to control ‖Eh⁢Ph⁢S⁢(tn)⁢x−S⁢(tn)⁢x‖X\left\|E_{h}P_{h}S(t^{n})x-S(t^{n})x\right\|_{X} uniformly in nn. The orbit {S⁢(t)⁢x:0≤t≤T}\{S(t)x:0\leq t\leq T\} is compact by strong continuity. The operators Eh⁢PhE_{h}P_{h} have norms at most one and converge strongly to II, so convergence is uniform on this compact orbit: approximate the orbit by a finite ε\varepsilon-net and use the uniform operator bound. This proves (6.22) for x∈Dx\in D. For arbitrary x∈Xx\in X, choose d∈Dd\in D close to xx. Uniform stability and the continuous bound give

maxn⁡‖Eh⁢Ghn⁢Ph⁢x−S⁢(tn)⁢x‖X\displaystyle\max_{n}\left\|E_{h}G_{h}^{n}P_{h}x-S(t^{n})x\right\|_{X} ≤(CT+MT)⁢‖x−d‖X\displaystyle\leq(C_{T}+M_{T})\left\|x-d\right\|_{X} (6.23)
+maxn⁡‖Eh⁢Ghn⁢Ph⁢d−S⁢(tn)⁢d‖X.\displaystyle\quad+\max_{n}\left\|E_{h}G_{h}^{n}P_{h}d-S(t^{n})d\right\|_{X}. (6.24)

First let h→0h\to 0, then let d→xd\to x.

Convergence implies stability. Fix T>0T>0. If stability failed for every sufficiently small range of parameters, we could select hm→0h_{m}\to 0 and 0≤nm⁢Δ⁢t⁢(hm)≤T0\leq n_{m}\Delta t(h_{m})\leq T such that ‖Ghmnm‖Xhm→Xhm>m\left\|G_{h_{m}}^{n_{m}}\right\|_{X_{h_{m}}\to X_{h_{m}}}>m. Set Am=Ehm⁢Ghmnm⁢Phm∈ℒ⁢(X,X)A_{m}=E_{h_{m}}G_{h_{m}}^{n_{m}}P_{h_{m}}\in\mathcal{L}(X,X). For each x∈Xx\in X, convergence and the bound MTM_{T} imply that (Am⁢x)(A_{m}x) is bounded. Banach–Steinhaus therefore yields supm‖Am‖X→X<+∞\sup_{m}\left\|A_{m}\right\|_{X\to X}<+\infty. But (6.20) implies

‖Am‖X→X=‖Ghmnm‖Xhm→Xhm:\left\|A_{m}\right\|_{X\to X}=\left\|G_{h_{m}}^{n_{m}}\right\|_{X_{h_{m}}\to X% _{h_{m}}}: (6.25)

one inequality follows from the bounds for Ph,EhP_{h},E_{h}, and the other by testing AmA_{m} on Ehm⁢VE_{h_{m}}V, since Phm⁢Ehm⁢V=VP_{h_{m}}E_{h_{m}}V=V. This contradicts the choice of nm,hmn_{m},h_{m}. ∎

Remark 6.16.

This is an evolution form of the Lax–Richtmyer theorem; see [1]. Consistency need only be checked on a dense smooth class, whereas the convergence assertion quantifies over all data in the ambient Banach space. Failure of stability rules out this general convergence assertion. It does not rule out convergence for particular initial data, such as a finite combination of fixed Fourier modes.
