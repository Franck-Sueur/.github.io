Chapter 1 The Lax–Milgram theorem and Stampacchia’s extension

1.1 Motivation: the Poisson equation

Consider first the one-dimensional Dirichlet problem

−u′′=fin ⁢(0,1),u⁢(0)=u⁢(1)=0.-u^{\prime\prime}=f\quad\text{in }(0,1),\qquad u(0)=u(1)=0.

If uu is a smooth solution and vv is a smooth test function vanishing at both endpoints, integration by parts gives

∫01u′⁢v′⁢𝑑x=∫01f⁢v⁢𝑑x.\int_{0}^{1}u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}fv\,dx.

This formulation uses only first derivatives. It also has an energy interpretation: the equation is the first-order condition for the functional

F⁢(v)=12⁢∫01|v′|2⁢𝑑x−∫01f⁢v⁢𝑑x.F(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}|v^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx-\int_{0}^{1}fv\,dx.

Chapter 2 will provide the complete Hilbert space H01⁢(0,1)H^{1}_{0}(0,1) in which these identities make sense, and Chapter 3 will apply the abstract theorem below to this and other elliptic problems. The key questions are already visible: when is the bilinear form continuous, when does it control the chosen norm, and is the right-hand side continuous?

We begin with the equation on a Hilbert space, which is the main tool for the elliptic and finite element chapters. We then extend it to variational inequalities on closed convex constraint sets.

1.2 Setting, notation, and the Lax–Milgram theorem

Throughout, HH is a real Hilbert space with scalar product (⋅,⋅)H\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{H} and norm

‖u‖H=(u,u)H1/2.\left\|u\right\|_{H}=\left(u,u\right)_{H}^{1/2}.

Its topological dual is denoted by H′H^{\prime}, and duality pairings are written as ⟨⋅,⋅⟩H′,H\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}. The point of the indices is to avoid confusing the Hilbert scalar product with a duality pairing.

Definition 1.1.

Let a:H×H→ℝa:H\times H\to\mathbb{R} be a bilinear form.

  1. (i)

    It is continuous if there exists Ca>0C_{a}>0 such that

    |a⁢(u,v)|≤Ca⁢‖u‖H⁢‖v‖H∀u,v∈H.|a(u,v)|\leq C_{a}\left\|u\right\|_{H}\,\left\|v\right\|_{H}\qquad\forall u,v% \in H.
  2. (ii)

    It is coercive if there exists α>0\alpha>0 such that

    a⁢(v,v)≥α⁢‖v‖H2∀v∈H.a(v,v)\geq\alpha\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}\qquad\forall v\in H.

We shall repeatedly use the Riesz representation theorem on HH: every continuous linear functional ℓ∈H′\ell\in H^{\prime} is represented in a unique way by an element f∈Hf\in H such that

⟨ℓ,v⟩H′,H=(f,v)H∀v∈H.\left\langle\ell,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}=\left(f,v\right)_{H}\qquad% \forall v\in H.
Theorem 1.2 (Lax–Milgram).

Let a:H×H→ℝa:H\times H\to\mathbb{R} be a continuous coercive bilinear form. For every φ∈H′\varphi\in H^{\prime}, there exists a unique u∈Hu\in H such that

a⁢(u,v)=⟨φ,v⟩H′,H∀v∈H.a(u,v)=\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall v\in H. (1.1)

If aa is symmetric, then uu is the unique minimizer on HH of

F⁢(v)=12⁢a⁢(v,v)−⟨φ,v⟩H′,H.F(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)-\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}.

1.3 An operator proof of the Lax–Milgram theorem

The first part of the theorem follows by representing the bilinear form and the right-hand side with the Hilbert scalar product.

By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a unique element f∈Hf\in H such that

⟨φ,v⟩H′,H=(f,v)H∀v∈H.\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}=\left(f,v\right)_{H}\qquad% \forall v\in H. (1.2)

Fix u∈Hu\in H. Since aa is continuous, the map

H∋v⟼a⁢(u,v)∈ℝH\ni v\longmapsto a(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}

is a continuous linear functional on HH. By Riesz, there exists a unique vector A⁢u∈HAu\in H such that

a⁢(u,v)=(A⁢u,v)H∀v∈H.a(u,v)=\left(Au,v\right)_{H}\qquad\forall v\in H. (1.3)

In this way we define a linear operator A:H→HA:H\to H. It enjoys three key properties:

  1. (a)

    Boundedness. For every u∈Hu\in H,

    ‖A⁢u‖H≤Ca⁢‖u‖H.\left\|Au\right\|_{H}\leq C_{a}\left\|u\right\|_{H}. (1.4)

    Indeed, by (1.3) and continuity of aa,

    |(A⁢u,v)H|=|a⁢(u,v)|≤Ca⁢‖u‖H⁢‖v‖H∀v∈H.|\left(Au,v\right)_{H}|=|a(u,v)|\leq C_{a}\left\|u\right\|_{H}\,\left\|v\right% \|_{H}\qquad\forall v\in H.

    Taking the supremum over ‖v‖H=1\left\|v\right\|_{H}=1 yields (1.4).

  2. (b)

    Coercivity in operator form. For every u∈Hu\in H,

    (A⁢u,u)H=a⁢(u,u)≥α⁢‖u‖H2.\left(Au,u\right)_{H}=a(u,u)\geq\alpha\left\|u\right\|_{H}^{2}. (1.5)
  3. (c)

    Strong monotonicity. For every u,v∈Hu,v\in H,

    (A⁢u−A⁢v,u−v)H≥α⁢‖u−v‖H2.\left(Au-Av,u-v\right)_{H}\geq\alpha\left\|u-v\right\|_{H}^{2}. (1.6)

    This is just coercivity applied to u−vu-v:

    (A⁢u−A⁢v,u−v)H=a⁢(u−v,u−v)≥α⁢‖u−v‖H2.\left(Au-Av,u-v\right)_{H}=a(u-v,u-v)\geq\alpha\left\|u-v\right\|_{H}^{2}.

With the operator defined by (1.3), the equation (1.1) is equivalent to

A⁢u=f,Au=f, (1.7)

where f∈Hf\in H is the Riesz representative of φ\varphi, that is, ⟨φ,v⟩H′,H=(f,v)H\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}=\left(f,v\right)_{H} for all v∈Hv\in H.

Proposition 1.3.

The operator A:H→HA:H\to H is bijective. Consequently, for every φ∈H′\varphi\in H^{\prime}, equation (1.1) has a unique solution.

Proof.

We prove injectivity, closedness of the range, and density of the range.

(a) Injectivity. If A⁢u=0Au=0, then by coercivity,

α⁢‖u‖H2≤(A⁢u,u)H=0.\alpha\left\|u\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left(Au,u\right)_{H}=0.

Hence u=0u=0, so AA is injective.

(b) The range R⁢(A)R(A) is closed. First observe that coercivity implies the lower bound

α⁢‖v‖H≤‖A⁢v‖H∀v∈H.\alpha\left\|v\right\|_{H}\leq\left\|Av\right\|_{H}\qquad\forall v\in H. (1.8)

Indeed, from (1.5) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

α⁢‖v‖H2≤(A⁢v,v)H≤‖A⁢v‖H⁢‖v‖H.\alpha\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left(Av,v\right)_{H}\leq\left\|Av\right\|_{% H}\,\left\|v\right\|_{H}.

If v≠0v\neq 0, dividing by ‖v‖H\left\|v\right\|_{H} gives (1.8); if v=0v=0, it is trivial.

Now let (fn)⊂R⁢(A)(f_{n})\subset R(A) be a sequence converging in HH to some f∈Hf\in H. Write fn=A⁢unf_{n}=Au_{n} with un∈Hu_{n}\in H. We claim that (un)(u_{n}) is a Cauchy sequence. Indeed, applying (1.8) to un−umu_{n}-u_{m}, we find

α⁢‖un−um‖H≤‖A⁢(un−um)‖H=‖fn−fm‖H.\alpha\left\|u_{n}-u_{m}\right\|_{H}\leq\left\|A(u_{n}-u_{m})\right\|_{H}=% \left\|f_{n}-f_{m}\right\|_{H}.

Since (fn)(f_{n}) converges in HH, it is Cauchy; therefore (un)(u_{n}) is also Cauchy. Because HH is complete, un→uu_{n}\to u for some u∈Hu\in H. By the continuity of AA,

A⁢u=limn→∞A⁢un=limn→∞fn=f.Au=\lim_{n\to\infty}Au_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}=f.

Hence f∈R⁢(A)f\in R(A), so R⁢(A)R(A) is closed.

(c) The range R⁢(A)R(A) is dense. Suppose v∈Hv\in H is orthogonal to R⁢(A)R(A), meaning that

(A⁢u,v)H=0∀u∈H.\left(Au,v\right)_{H}=0\qquad\forall u\in H. (1.9)

Taking u=vu=v in (1.9) gives

(A⁢v,v)H=0.\left(Av,v\right)_{H}=0.

By coercivity,

α⁢‖v‖H2≤(A⁢v,v)H=0,\alpha\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left(Av,v\right)_{H}=0,

so v=0v=0. Therefore

R⁢(A)⟂={0}.R(A)^{\perp}=\{0\}.

In a Hilbert space, Section 1.9 shows that for every linear subspace M⊂HM\subset H,

M¯=(M⟂)⟂.\overline{M}=(M^{\perp})^{\perp}.

Applying this to M=R⁢(A)M=R(A), we obtain

R⁢(A)¯=(R⁢(A)⟂)⟂={0}⟂=H.\overline{R(A)}=(R(A)^{\perp})^{\perp}=\{0\}^{\perp}=H.

So R⁢(A)R(A) is dense in HH. Since it is also closed by part (b), we conclude that R⁢(A)=HR(A)=H.

The operator AA is therefore bijective. Solving A⁢u=fAu=f is the same as solving (1.1), so the Lax–Milgram theorem follows. ∎

The solution also satisfies the estimate

‖u‖H≤1α⁢‖φ‖H′.\left\|u\right\|_{H}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{H^{\prime}}.

Indeed, taking v=uv=u in (1.1) gives α⁢‖u‖H2≤⟨φ,u⟩H′,H≤‖φ‖H′⁢‖u‖H\alpha\left\|u\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left\langle\varphi,u\right\rangle_{H^{% \prime},H}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{H^{\prime}}\left\|u\right\|_{H}; division by ‖u‖H\left\|u\right\|_{H} proves the estimate when u≠0u\neq 0, and the case u=0u=0 is immediate.

1.4 The symmetric case: energy minimization

Assume now that aa is symmetric. Define a new scalar product on the underlying vector space HH by

(u,v)a:=a⁢(u,v),‖u‖a:=(u,u)a1/2=a⁢(u,u)1/2.\left(u,v\right)_{a}:=a(u,v),\qquad\left\|u\right\|_{a}:=\left(u,u\right)_{a}^% {1/2}=a(u,u)^{1/2}.

Because aa is symmetric, continuous, and coercive,

α⁢‖u‖H≤‖u‖a≤Ca⁢‖u‖H∀u∈H.\sqrt{\alpha}\,\left\|u\right\|_{H}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{a}\leq\sqrt{C_{a}}\,% \left\|u\right\|_{H}\qquad\forall u\in H. (1.10)

Thus ∥⋅∥a\left\|\cdot\right\|_{a} is equivalent to ∥⋅∥H\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H}, so the same vector space becomes another Hilbert space, which we denote by HaH_{a}.

Since the two norms are equivalent, the functional φ\varphi is continuous both on HH and on HaH_{a}. By the Riesz representation theorem applied to HaH_{a}, there exists a unique g∈Hg\in H such that

⟨φ,v⟩Ha′,Ha=(g,v)a=a⁢(g,v)∀v∈H.\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H_{a}^{\prime},H_{a}}=\left(g,v\right)_{a}% =a(g,v)\qquad\forall v\in H. (1.11)

The vector gg is the unique solution of (1.1). Expanding the square gives

‖g−v‖a2=a⁢(v,v)−2⁢⟨φ,v⟩H′,H+a⁢(g,g)=2⁢F⁢(v)+a⁢(g,g),F⁢(v)=12⁢a⁢(v,v)−⟨φ,v⟩H′,H.\left\|g-v\right\|_{a}^{2}=a(v,v)-2\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{% \prime},H}+a(g,g)=2F(v)+a(g,g),\qquad F(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)-\left\langle% \varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}.

Since the squared distance from gg is uniquely minimized at v=gv=g on HH, the Lax–Milgram solution is the unique minimizer of FF. Equivalently, if uu denotes this solution, then

F⁢(v)−F⁢(u)=12⁢a⁢(v−u,v−u)≥α2⁢‖v−u‖H2∀v∈H.F(v)-F(u)=\frac{1}{2}a(v-u,v-u)\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|v-u\right\|_{H}^{2}% \qquad\forall v\in H.

This proves the minimization assertion of Theorem 1.2. It also shows why symmetry matters: in general the derivative of 12⁢a⁢(v,v)\frac{1}{2}a(v,v) involves the symmetric part of aa, rather than aa itself.

1.5 Stampacchia’s extension to convex constraints

On a closed convex set K⊂HK\subset H, not every direction and its negative are admissible. The appropriate first-order condition is a variational inequality. For example, a positivity constraint describes an obstacle that a displacement must stay above. The following theorem extends Lax–Milgram to this setting.

Theorem 1.4.

Let a:H×H→ℝa:H\times H\to\mathbb{R} be a continuous coercive bilinear form, and let K⊂HK\subset H be nonempty, closed, and convex. Given φ∈H′\varphi\in H^{\prime}, there exists a unique u∈Ku\in K such that

a⁢(u,v−u)≥⟨φ,v−u⟩H′,H∀v∈K.a(u,v-u)\geq\left\langle\varphi,v-u\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall v% \in K. (1.12)

If, in addition, aa is symmetric, then the same uu is characterized by the minimization property

F⁢(u)=minv∈K⁡F⁢(v),F⁢(v):=12⁢a⁢(v,v)−⟨φ,v⟩H′,H.F(u)=\min_{v\in K}F(v),\qquad F(v):=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)-\left\langle\varphi,v% \right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}. (1.13)

The classical auxiliary results used in the proof are recalled later in this chapter:

1.6 Proof of Stampacchia in five steps

1.6.1 Step 1: Reformulation with scalar products.

Let ff be the Riesz representative already defined in (1.2). Hence the variational inequality (1.12) can already be rewritten as

a⁢(u,v−u)≥(f,v−u)H∀v∈K.a(u,v-u)\geq\left(f,v-u\right)_{H}\qquad\forall v\in K. (1.14)

The advantage is that the right-hand side is now expressed using the Hilbert scalar product on HH.

1.6.2 Step 2: Operator form.

Use the operator AA defined in (1.3). Its boundedness, coercivity, and strong monotonicity were established in (1.4)–(1.6). Using (1.3) and (1.2), the problem (1.12) is therefore equivalent to

(A⁢u−f,v−u)H≥0∀v∈K,\left(Au-f,v-u\right)_{H}\geq 0\qquad\forall v\in K, (1.15)

or, equivalently,

(f−A⁢u,v−u)H≤0∀v∈K.\left(f-Au,v-u\right)_{H}\leq 0\qquad\forall v\in K. (1.16)

1.6.3 Step 3: Fixed-point reformulation.

Let ρ>0\rho>0. Since

(u+ρ⁢(f−A⁢u)−u,v−u)H=ρ⁢(f−A⁢u,v−u)H,\left(u+\rho(f-Au)-u,v-u\right)_{H}=\rho\,\left(f-Au,v-u\right)_{H},

condition (1.16) is equivalent to

(u+ρ⁢(f−A⁢u)−u,v−u)H≤0∀v∈K.\left(u+\rho(f-Au)-u,v-u\right)_{H}\leq 0\qquad\forall v\in K. (1.17)

By the characterization of the projection onto a closed convex set (Theorem 1.5 in Section 1.9), (1.17) is equivalent to

u=PKH⁢(u+ρ⁢(f−A⁢u)).u=P_{K}^{H}\bigl{(}u+\rho(f-Au)\bigr{)}. (1.18)

Thus, if we define

Sρ⁢(v):=PKH⁢(v+ρ⁢(f−A⁢v))(v∈K),S_{\rho}(v):=P_{K}^{H}\bigl{(}v+\rho(f-Av)\bigr{)}\qquad(v\in K),

then solving (1.12) is exactly the same as finding a fixed point of Sρ:K→KS_{\rho}:K\to K.

1.6.4 Step 4: Contracting property.

Take v1,v2∈Kv_{1},v_{2}\in K. Since the projection PKHP_{K}^{H} is nonexpansive (Proposition 1.6),

‖Sρ⁢(v1)−Sρ⁢(v2)‖H≤‖(v1−v2)−ρ⁢(A⁢v1−A⁢v2)‖H.\left\|S_{\rho}(v_{1})-S_{\rho}(v_{2})\right\|_{H}\leq\left\|(v_{1}-v_{2})-% \rho(Av_{1}-Av_{2})\right\|_{H}.

Set w=v1−v2w=v_{1}-v_{2}. Then

‖w−ρ⁢A⁢w‖H2\displaystyle\left\|w-\rho Aw\right\|_{H}^{2} =‖w‖H2−2⁢ρ⁢(A⁢w,w)H+ρ2⁢‖A⁢w‖H2\displaystyle=\left\|w\right\|_{H}^{2}-2\rho\,\left(Aw,w\right)_{H}+\rho^{2}% \left\|Aw\right\|_{H}^{2}
≤‖w‖H2−2⁢ρ⁢α⁢‖w‖H2+ρ2⁢Ca2⁢‖w‖H2\displaystyle\leq\left\|w\right\|_{H}^{2}-2\rho\alpha\left\|w\right\|_{H}^{2}+% \rho^{2}C_{a}^{2}\left\|w\right\|_{H}^{2}
=(1−2⁢α⁢ρ+Ca2⁢ρ2)⁢‖w‖H2,\displaystyle=\bigl{(}1-2\alpha\rho+C_{a}^{2}\rho^{2}\bigr{)}\left\|w\right\|_% {H}^{2},

where we used (1.5) and (1.4). Consequently,

‖Sρ⁢(v1)−Sρ⁢(v2)‖H≤q⁢‖v1−v2‖H,q:=1−2⁢α⁢ρ+Ca2⁢ρ2.\left\|S_{\rho}(v_{1})-S_{\rho}(v_{2})\right\|_{H}\leq q\,\left\|v_{1}-v_{2}% \right\|_{H},\qquad q:=\sqrt{1-2\alpha\rho+C_{a}^{2}\rho^{2}}. (1.19)

If

0<ρ<2⁢αCa2,0<\rho<\frac{2\alpha}{C_{a}^{2}}, (1.20)

then q<1q<1, so SρS_{\rho} is a strict contraction on the complete metric space KK (closed subsets of Hilbert spaces are complete). By Banach’s fixed-point theorem (Section 1.10), SρS_{\rho} has a unique fixed point. By (1.18), this fixed point is exactly the unique solution u∈Ku\in K of (1.12).

For numerical purposes, the upper bound in (1.19) is minimized by the choice ρ=α/Ca2\rho=\alpha/C_{a}^{2}.

1.6.5 Step 5: The symmetric case and the energy scalar product.

Assume now that aa is symmetric. Use the equivalent Hilbert structure HaH_{a} of Section 1.4 and the vector gg characterized by (1.11). The variational inequality (1.12) becomes

(u,v−u)a≥(g,v−u)a∀v∈K,\left(u,v-u\right)_{a}\geq\left(g,v-u\right)_{a}\qquad\forall v\in K,

that is,

(g−u,v−u)a≤0∀v∈K.\left(g-u,v-u\right)_{a}\leq 0\qquad\forall v\in K. (1.21)

Because KK is convex and closed for ∥⋅∥H\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H}, it is also convex and closed for ∥⋅∥a\left\|\cdot\right\|_{a} by norm equivalence. Hence, by the projection theorem in the Hilbert space HaH_{a}, (1.21) is equivalent to

u=PKa⁢g,u=P_{K}^{a}g,

where PKaP_{K}^{a} denotes projection onto KK with respect to the scalar product (⋅,⋅)a\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{a}. Therefore uu is the unique minimizer of

v⟼‖g−v‖a2=(g−v,g−v)aover ⁢K.v\longmapsto\left\|g-v\right\|_{a}^{2}=\left(g-v,g-v\right)_{a}\qquad\text{% over }K.

Expanding this quantity and using (1.11), we get

(g−v,g−v)a=a⁢(v,v)−2⁢a⁢(g,v)+a⁢(g,g)=a⁢(v,v)−2⁢⟨φ,v⟩Ha′,Ha+a⁢(g,g).\left(g-v,g-v\right)_{a}=a(v,v)-2a(g,v)+a(g,g)=a(v,v)-2\left\langle\varphi,v% \right\rangle_{H_{a}^{\prime},H_{a}}+a(g,g).

The constant term a⁢(g,g)a(g,g) is irrelevant for minimization, so minimizing ‖g−v‖a2\left\|g-v\right\|_{a}^{2} over KK is equivalent to minimizing

F⁢(v)=12⁢a⁢(v,v)−⟨φ,v⟩H′,Hover ⁢K.F(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)-\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad% \text{over }K.

This proves (1.13).

1.7 Recovering Lax–Milgram from Stampacchia

The theorem on the whole Hilbert space can also be recovered as the special case K=HK=H of Theorem 1.4. This gives a second proof of Theorem 1.2 and clarifies the distinction between an equation and a constrained variational inequality.

Proof.

Apply Theorem 1.4 with K=HK=H. Since HH is closed and convex in itself, there exists a unique u∈Hu\in H such that

a⁢(u,v−u)≥⟨φ,v−u⟩H′,H∀v∈H.a(u,v-u)\geq\left\langle\varphi,v-u\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall v% \in H. (1.22)

The key point is that now every direction is admissible, with both signs. Take an arbitrary w∈Hw\in H. Choosing v=u+wv=u+w in (1.22) gives

a⁢(u,w)≥⟨φ,w⟩H′,H.a(u,w)\geq\left\langle\varphi,w\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}. (1.23)

Choosing instead v=u−wv=u-w gives

a⁢(u,−w)≥⟨φ,−w⟩H′,H,a(u,-w)\geq\left\langle\varphi,-w\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H},

that is,

a⁢(u,w)≤⟨φ,w⟩H′,H.a(u,w)\leq\left\langle\varphi,w\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}. (1.24)

Combining (1.23) and (1.24), we obtain

a⁢(u,w)=⟨φ,w⟩H′,H∀w∈H,a(u,w)=\left\langle\varphi,w\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall w\in H,

which is exactly (1.1).

Uniqueness follows either from the uniqueness part of Theorem 1.4 or directly from coercivity: if u1u_{1} and u2u_{2} both satisfy (1.1), then

a⁢(u1−u2,v)=0∀v∈H.a(u_{1}-u_{2},v)=0\qquad\forall v\in H.

Taking v=u1−u2v=u_{1}-u_{2} yields

α⁢‖u1−u2‖H2≤a⁢(u1−u2,u1−u2)=0,\alpha\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{H}^{2}\leq a(u_{1}-u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})=0,

so u1=u2u_{1}=u_{2}.

When aa is symmetric, Theorem 1.4 with K=HK=H gives the minimization property immediately. ∎

1.8 The functional FF and its derivative

Assume in this section that aa is symmetric, and define

F⁢(v)=12⁢a⁢(v,v)−⟨φ,v⟩H′,H(v∈H).F(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)-\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad% (v\in H).

Section 1.11 recalls the definitions of Gâteaux and Fréchet derivatives and proves that FF is actually Fréchet differentiable, with

D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[h]=a⁢(u,h)−⟨φ,h⟩H′,H∀u,h∈H.DF(u)[h]=a(u,h)-\left\langle\varphi,h\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall u% ,h\in H. (1.25)

The remainder term is especially simple:

F⁢(u+h)−F⁢(u)−D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[h]=12⁢a⁢(h,h).F(u+h)-F(u)-DF(u)[h]=\frac{1}{2}a(h,h). (1.26)

Because a⁢(h,h)≥α⁢‖h‖H2a(h,h)\geq\alpha\left\|h\right\|_{H}^{2}, the functional FF is strictly convex; in fact it is strongly convex.

Formula (1.25) makes the variational meaning of the previous results completely transparent:

  • •

    The unconstrained equation (1.1) is exactly the condition D⁢F⁢(u)=0DF(u)=0 in H′H^{\prime}.

  • •

    On a closed convex set KK, the Stampacchia condition (1.12) is equivalent to

    D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[v−u]≥0∀v∈K,DF(u)[v-u]\geq 0\qquad\forall v\in K,

    which is the standard first-order optimality condition for minimizing a differentiable convex functional on a convex set.

Indeed, using (1.26) with h=v−uh=v-u, we obtain the exact identity

F⁢(v)−F⁢(u)=D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[v−u]+12⁢a⁢(v−u,v−u).F(v)-F(u)=DF(u)[v-u]+\frac{1}{2}a(v-u,v-u).

Hence, if D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[v−u]≥0DF(u)[v-u]\geq 0 for all v∈Kv\in K, then automatically F⁢(v)≥F⁢(u)F(v)\geq F(u) for all v∈Kv\in K. Conversely, if uu minimizes FF on KK, then the one-variable function t↦F⁢(u+t⁢(v−u))t\mapsto F(u+t(v-u)) has a minimum at t=0t=0 on [0,1][0,1], so its right derivative at 0 is nonnegative, which is exactly D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[v−u]≥0DF(u)[v-u]\geq 0.

1.9 Projection onto a closed convex set

In this section (X,(⋅,⋅)X)(X,(\cdot,\cdot)_{X}) denotes a real Hilbert space with norm ∥⋅∥X\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}. We keep the notation generic so that the result can be applied both to (H,(⋅,⋅)H)(H,\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{H}) and to (Ha,(⋅,⋅)a)(H_{a},\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{a}).

Theorem 1.5.

Let C⊂XC\subset X be nonempty, closed, and convex. Then for every z∈Xz\in X there exists a unique element u∈Cu\in C such that

‖z−u‖X=dist⁡(z,C):=infv∈C‖z−v‖X.\left\|z-u\right\|_{X}=\operatorname{dist}(z,C):=\inf_{v\in C}\left\|z-v\right% \|_{X}.

‖z−un+um2‖X2+‖un−um2‖X2=12⁢(‖z−un‖X2+‖z−um‖X2).\left\|z-\frac{u_{n}+u_{m}}{2}\right\|_{X}^{2}+\left\|\frac{u_{n}-u_{m}}{2}% \right\|_{X}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl{(}\left\|z-u_{n}\right\|_{X}^{2}+\left\|z-u_% {m}\right\|_{X}^{2}\bigr{)}.

‖z−un+um2‖X2≥d2.\left\|z-\frac{u_{n}+u_{m}}{2}\right\|_{X}^{2}\geq d^{2}.

Therefore

‖un−um2‖X2≤12⁢(‖z−un‖X2+‖z−um‖X2)−d2→n,m→∞0.\left\|\frac{u_{n}-u_{m}}{2}\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\bigl{(}\left\|z-u_% {n}\right\|_{X}^{2}+\left\|z-u_{m}\right\|_{X}^{2}\bigr{)}-d^{2}\xrightarrow[n% ,m\to\infty]{}0.

Expanding the square gives

0≤−2⁢t⁢(z−u,v−u)X+t2⁢‖v−u‖X2.0\leq-2t(z-u,v-u)_{X}+t^{2}\left\|v-u\right\|_{X}^{2}.

Dividing by t>0t>0 and letting t↓0t\downarrow 0 yields (1.27).

Conversely, assume that (1.27) holds. Then for every v∈Cv\in C,

‖z−v‖X2\displaystyle\left\|z-v\right\|_{X}^{2} =‖(z−u)−(v−u)‖X2\displaystyle=\left\|(z-u)-(v-u)\right\|_{X}^{2}
=‖z−u‖X2−2⁢(z−u,v−u)X+‖v−u‖X2\displaystyle=\left\|z-u\right\|_{X}^{2}-2(z-u,v-u)_{X}+\left\|v-u\right\|_{X}% ^{2}
≥‖z−u‖X2.\displaystyle\geq\left\|z-u\right\|_{X}^{2}.

Thus uu is indeed a minimizer.

Uniqueness. If u1,u2∈Cu_{1},u_{2}\in C both satisfy (1.27), then choosing v=u2v=u_{2} in the inequality for u1u_{1}, and v=u1v=u_{1} in the inequality for u2u_{2}, gives

‖u1−u2‖X2≤0.\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq 0.

Hence u1=u2u_{1}=u_{2}. ∎

Proposition 1.6.

Let C⊂XC\subset X be nonempty, closed, and convex. Then

‖PCX⁢z1−PCX⁢z2‖X≤‖z1−z2‖X∀z1,z2∈X.\left\|P_{C}^{X}z_{1}-P_{C}^{X}z_{2}\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|z_{1}-z_{2}\right\|% _{X}\qquad\forall z_{1},z_{2}\in X.

Set ui=PCX⁢ziu_{i}=P_{C}^{X}z_{i} for i=1,2i=1,2. By (1.27),

(z1−u1,u2−u1)X≤0,(z2−u2,u1−u2)X≤0.(z_{1}-u_{1},u_{2}-u_{1})_{X}\leq 0,\qquad(z_{2}-u_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})_{X}\leq 0.

‖u1−u2‖X2≤(z1−z2,u1−u2)X.\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq(z_{1}-z_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})_{X}.

‖u1−u2‖X2≤‖z1−z2‖X⁢‖u1−u2‖X,\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{X}^{2}\leq\left\|z_{1}-z_{2}\right\|_{X}\,\left\|u% _{1}-u_{2}\right\|_{X},

Corollary 1.7.

1.10 Banach’s fixed-point theorem

Theorem 1.8.

Let (X,d)(X,d) be a nonempty complete metric space, and let S:X→XS:X\to X satisfy

d⁢(S⁢x,S⁢y)≤k⁢d⁢(x,y)∀x,y∈X,d(Sx,Sy)\leq k\,d(x,y)\qquad\forall x,y\in X,

for some constant 0≤k<10\leq k<1. Then SS has a unique fixed point in XX.

Proof.

Choose x0∈Xx_{0}\in X and define the Picard iterates by xn+1=S⁢xnx_{n+1}=Sx_{n}. Then

Since SS is Lipschitz, it is continuous. Therefore

Because k<1k<1, this forces d⁢(u,v)=0d(u,v)=0, hence u=vu=v. ∎

1.11 Gâteaux and Fréchet derivatives

Definition 1.9.

Let EE be a Banach space, U⊂EU\subset E an open set, and G:U→ℝG:U\to\mathbb{R}. We say that GG is Gâteaux differentiable at u∈Uu\in U if for every h∈Eh\in E the limit

δ⁢G⁢(u;h):=limt→0G⁢(u+t⁢h)−G⁢(u)t\delta G(u;h):=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{G(u+th)-G(u)}{t}

exists, and if the map h↦δ⁢G⁢(u;h)h\mapsto\delta G(u;h) is linear and continuous. This linear functional is denoted by D⁢G⁢(u)DG(u).

Definition 1.10.

Let EE, UU, and GG be as above. We say that GG is Fréchet differentiable at u∈Uu\in U if there exists a bounded linear functional L∈E′L\in E^{\prime} such that

lim‖h‖E→0|G⁢(u+h)−G⁢(u)−L⁢(h)|‖h‖E=0.\lim_{\left\|h\right\|_{E}\to 0}\frac{|G(u+h)-G(u)-L(h)|}{\left\|h\right\|_{E}% }=0.

The functional LL is then written D⁢G⁢(u)DG(u).

Remark 1.11.

Fréchet differentiability is stronger than Gâteaux differentiability. If GG is Fréchet differentiable at uu, then for every direction hh the one-variable expansion

G⁢(u+t⁢h)=G⁢(u)+t⁢D⁢G⁢(u)⁢[h]+o⁢(|t|)G(u+th)=G(u)+t\,DG(u)[h]+o(|t|)

holds as t→0t\to 0. In particular, the Gâteaux derivative exists and equals the Fréchet derivative.

Proposition 1.12.

Assume that a:H×H→ℝa:H\times H\to\mathbb{R} is continuous and symmetric, and let φ∈H′\varphi\in H^{\prime}. Define

F⁢(v)=12⁢a⁢(v,v)−⟨φ,v⟩H′,H.F(v)=\frac{1}{2}a(v,v)-\left\langle\varphi,v\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}.

Then FF is Fréchet differentiable on HH, and

D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[h]=a⁢(u,h)−⟨φ,h⟩H′,H∀u,h∈H.DF(u)[h]=a(u,h)-\left\langle\varphi,h\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}\qquad\forall u% ,h\in H. (1.30)

Moreover,

F⁢(u+h)−F⁢(u)−D⁢F⁢(u)⁢[h]=12⁢a⁢(h,h),F(u+h)-F(u)-DF(u)[h]=\frac{1}{2}a(h,h), (1.31)

Proof.

Because aa is symmetric,

F⁢(u+h)−F⁢(u)\displaystyle F(u+h)-F(u) =12⁢(a⁢(u+h,u+h)−a⁢(u,u))−⟨φ,h⟩H′,H\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigl{(}a(u+h,u+h)-a(u,u)\bigr{)}-\left\langle\varphi% ,h\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}
=a⁢(u,h)+12⁢a⁢(h,h)−⟨φ,h⟩H′,H.\displaystyle=a(u,h)+\frac{1}{2}a(h,h)-\left\langle\varphi,h\right\rangle_{H^{% \prime},H}.

Thus the natural candidate for the derivative is

Lu⁢(h)=a⁢(u,h)−⟨φ,h⟩H′,H.L_{u}(h)=a(u,h)-\left\langle\varphi,h\right\rangle_{H^{\prime},H}.

This is a bounded linear functional of hh, and the previous identity becomes

F⁢(u+h)−F⁢(u)−Lu⁢(h)=12⁢a⁢(h,h).F(u+h)-F(u)-L_{u}(h)=\frac{1}{2}a(h,h).

By continuity of aa,

|12⁢a⁢(h,h)|≤Ca2⁢‖h‖H2=o⁢(‖h‖H)as ⁢‖h‖H→0.\left|\frac{1}{2}a(h,h)\right|\leq\frac{C_{a}}{2}\left\|h\right\|_{H}^{2}=o(% \left\|h\right\|_{H})\qquad\text{as }\left\|h\right\|_{H}\to 0.

Therefore Lu=D⁢F⁢(u)L_{u}=DF(u), proving (1.30) and (1.31). ∎

Proposition 1.13.

Let EE be a Banach space, K⊂EK\subset E a convex set, and U⊂EU\subset E an open set containing KK. Let G:U→ℝG:U\to\mathbb{R} be Fréchet differentiable, with convex restriction to KK. Then, for u∈Ku\in K, the following are equivalent:

  1. (i)

    uu minimizes GG on KK;

  2. (ii)

    D⁢G⁢(u)⁢[v−u]≥0DG(u)[v-u]\geq 0 for every v∈Kv\in K.

Proof.

Assume first that uu minimizes GG on KK, and let v∈Kv\in K. Since KK is convex, the segment u+t⁢(v−u)u+t(v-u) lies in KK for every t∈[0,1]t\in[0,1]. Hence

ψ⁢(t):=G⁢(u+t⁢(v−u))\psi(t):=G\bigl{(}u+t(v-u)\bigr{)}

has a minimum at t=0t=0 on [0,1][0,1]. Therefore ψ′⁢(0+)≥0\psi^{\prime}(0^{+})\geq 0, i.e.

D⁢G⁢(u)⁢[v−u]≥0.DG(u)[v-u]\geq 0.

Conversely, assume that D⁢G⁢(u)⁢[v−u]≥0DG(u)[v-u]\geq 0 for every v∈Kv\in K. By convexity, for t∈(0,1)t\in(0,1),

G⁢(u+t⁢(v−u))≤(1−t)⁢G⁢(u)+t⁢G⁢(v).G\bigl{(}u+t(v-u)\bigr{)}\leq(1-t)G(u)+tG(v).

Since the left-hand side is nonnegative by assumption, G⁢(v)≥G⁢(u)G(v)\geq G(u) for every v∈Kv\in K. Thus uu minimizes GG on KK. ∎
